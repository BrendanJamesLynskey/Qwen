The lineup — eight models, two shapes

Qwen3.5 ships as two tiers — a "Small" set of dense models and a "Medium" set that mixes dense and sparse — all sharing one architecture family, one tokenizer, one training corpus, and Apache 2.0.

The efficiency claim in one comparison

The flagship activates 17B parameters per token. Competing open frontier models of the same era activate considerably more — Kimi K2.5 around 32B, GLM-5 around 40B. Qwen3.5 is betting that architecture beats brute activation, and the benchmark position (slide 06) says the bet largely paid off.

Every model in the list is natively multimodal and hybrid-attention. That uniformity is new: in the Qwen3 generation, vision meant a separate VL checkpoint and hybrid attention meant the one Qwen3-Next preview. Here it is the whole family, from 0.8B upward.

02

The flagship, specified

Qwen3.5-397B-A17B is Qwen3-Next's architecture, scaled. If you read Part 07, none of this will surprise you — which is the point.

03

Native multimodality by early fusion

Every previous Qwen vision model was built by late fusion: take a finished text LLM, bolt on a vision encoder and an adapter, and continue training. Qwen3.5 uses early fusion — visual tokens are in the mix from the start of pre-training.

Video is supported with configurable frame sampling, and the vision capability scales down the whole ladder — a Qwen3.5-2B reads documents, which is a materially different proposition from needing a separate 2B VL checkpoint for the job.

04

201 languages and a bigger vocabulary

Language coverage expanded from Qwen3's 119 to 201 languages and dialects — the broadest of any open model at release. That required breaking a tokenizer lineage that had held since 2023.

The change

The vocabulary grew from roughly 151,000 to about 250,000 entries (248,320 padded in the config). Qwen reports 10–60% better encoding efficiency across most languages — fewer tokens for the same text, which reduces cost, increases effective context and lowers attention cost quadratically for long documents.

The cost, and who pays it

A larger vocabulary means larger embedding and output-projection matrices. At 397B that is negligible. At 0.8B it is a meaningful fraction of the parameter budget — which is exactly the trade Qwen made in 2023 with a 152K vocabulary, made again at a larger scale. Tied embeddings on the small models absorb most of it.

What actually breaks when you upgrade

  • Token counts change. Every prompt-length estimate, cost model and context budget computed against a Qwen3 tokenizer is now wrong — usually in your favour, but wrong.
  • Cached tokenisations are invalid. Any pre-tokenised training corpus or embedding-chunk cache must be regenerated.
  • Fine-tunes do not transfer. A LoRA trained on a Qwen3 base cannot be applied to a Qwen3.5 base — different vocabulary, different embedding matrix, different everything.
  • Vocabulary-dependent tooling needs review. Custom logit processors, grammar constraints compiled against token IDs, and speculative-decoding draft models all need rebuilding.
Practical upgrade note

Treat Qwen3 → Qwen3.5 as a major version bump, not a point release. The name suggests continuity; the tokenizer says otherwise. Budget for re-tokenising corpora, re-training adapters and re-baselining your evaluations.

05

Thinking, finally reconciled

Part 05 ended with Qwen3 splitting its flagship into separate Instruct and Thinking checkpoints because one model could not hold both policies well. Qwen3.5 puts them back together — and this time it holds.

GenerationApproachOutcome
QwQ (2024–25)A dedicated reasoning model, separate from the general lineExcellent at hard reasoning, visibly worse at ordinary tasks
Qwen3 (Apr 2025)One checkpoint, mode selected per requestElegant, but each mode measurably compromised
Qwen3 2507 (Jul 2025)Split back into two checkpointsEach better in its lane; two deployments to run
Qwen3.5 (Feb 2026)One checkpoint, both modes, thinking on by defaultReported without the earlier quality tax

What is different this time

  • Far more capacity to spare. 397B of parameters with 512 experts gives the router room to allocate different experts to different regimes — the modes need not fight over the same weights the way they did in a 235B with 128 experts.
  • Reinforcement learning at a different scale. Qwen describes RL across "million-agent environments with progressively complex task distributions" — two orders of magnitude beyond Qwen3-Coder's 20,000 environments.
  • Thinking is the default, not the exception. The flagship generates a <think> prefix unless told otherwise. That inverts Qwen3's framing: reasoning is the normal mode and speed is the opt-out, which is a cleaner thing to post-train for.
Controlling it
# Thinking is on by default. Turn it off per request:
{ "chat_template_kwargs": { "enable_thinking": false } }

# Or server-wide, for a latency-sensitive endpoint:
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B --default-chat-template-kwargs '{"enable_thinking": false}'

# Sampling differs by mode — this matters more than people expect:
#   thinking:      temperature 0.6, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, min_p 0.0
#   non-thinking:  temperature 0.7, top_p 0.80, top_k 20, presence_penalty 1.5
#   max output:    32,768 general / 81,920 for complex problems

The most significant row

SWE-bench Verified 76.4. Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B — a model dedicated exclusively to coding, with 35B active parameters — scored 69.6 seven months earlier. The general model now beats the specialist at the specialist's own job, with half the active parameters. That is the clearest evidence in the family that the 2026 architecture is a real step change and not a marketing increment.

The independent view

Third-party evaluation put Qwen3.5-397B-A17B third among open-weight models on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at release (45), behind GLM-5 (50) and Kimi K2.5 (47) — while activating roughly half the parameters of either. Against its own predecessor Qwen3-235B the gains were large: reported improvements of around +361 Elo on agentic tasks, +27 on coding and +12 on scientific reasoning.

Alibaba Cloud pricing at launch was $0.60 / $3.60 per million input / output tokens — and, being Apache 2.0, self-hosting is always the alternative.

07

Where it is still weak

An honest deck needs this slide. Two weaknesses are documented and worth planning around.

Hallucination and calibration

On the AA-Omniscience index — which measures whether a model knows what it does not know — Qwen3.5 scored −32. That is a real improvement over Qwen3-235B's −48, but it remains behind the leading peers. The measured hallucination rate stayed around 88%; the index improved because accuracy rose to about 30%, not because the model refused more often.

In plain terms: it is more often right, but no more willing to say "I don't know." If your application cannot tolerate confident fabrication, you still need retrieval grounding, citation checking or a verification pass. This is not a Qwen-specific failing, but it is a specific, measured one.

Deployment weight of the flagship

397B of parameters must be resident regardless of how few are active — roughly 800GB at BF16, or 400GB at FP8. That is a multi-node deployment or a very large single node. The compute is cheap; the memory is not, and the 17B activation figure can mislead people into underestimating the hardware bill by an order of magnitude.

This is why the 122B-A10B and 35B-A3B exist, and why most teams should start there.

Two more worth knowing

  • Hybrid attention has a longer tail of runtime bugs. Gated DeltaNet kernels, hybrid KV-cache management and MTP are all newer code paths than plain attention. Expect to pin a known-good vLLM version rather than tracking latest, and expect occasional CUDA-graph capture issues that need --max-cudagraph-capture-size tuning.
  • Ecosystem lag on the small models. Every new architecture takes time to land in llama.cpp, MLX and the mobile runtimes. If your target is an embedded runtime rather than a GPU server, verify support before committing.
08

Serving Qwen3.5 with vLLM

vLLM has first-party recipes for the Qwen3.5 family. The minimum version is 0.17.0; hybrid attention, MTP and the multimodal path all need it.

The configurations you will actually use
# The everyday case: 9B dense, one 24GB card, full 262K context
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B \
  --max-model-len 262144 \
  --reasoning-parser qwen3

# Low-latency interactive: MTP self-speculation, thinking off
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B \
  --speculative-config '{"method": "mtp", "num_speculative_tokens": 1}' \
  --default-chat-template-kwargs '{"enable_thinking": false}' \
  --reasoning-parser qwen3

# Flagship across eight GPUs
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B \
  --port 8000 --tensor-parallel-size 8 --enable-expert-parallel \
  --max-model-len 262144 \
  --reasoning-parser qwen3

# Text-only workload: skip the vision tower and reclaim its memory
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B \
  --tensor-parallel-size 8 --max-model-len 262144 \
  --reasoning-parser qwen3 --language-model-only

# Agent backend: tool calls plus reasoning, both parsed out
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3.5-35B-A3B \
  --enable-auto-tool-choice --tool-call-parser qwen3_xml \
  --reasoning-parser qwen3

Qwen3.5 in the 2026 landscape

Qwen3.5 arrived into a field that had stopped agreeing about basic architecture. That disagreement is itself the story of 2026.

ModelAttention designTotal / activeThe bet
Qwen3.5Gated DeltaNet + gated attention, 3:1397B / 17BLinear attention is good enough for 75% of layers, and the efficiency pays for everything
GLM-5Multi-head latent attention + sparse attention— / ~40BCompress the KV cache rather than eliminate it
Kimi K2.5—~1T / ~32BExtreme total scale with aggressive sparsity
MiniMax-M2.5Plain multi-head attention— / ~17BFull attention everywhere, for predictability and reliability
Why Qwen's bet looks strong

Qwen3.5 matches or approaches the others' quality while activating the fewest parameters and holding the smallest KV cache. In a market where inference cost is the binding constraint on what you can actually ship, that is the axis that compounds. And the design has now been validated across four releases — Qwen3-Next, Qwen3-Coder-Next, Qwen3.5 and Qwen3.6 — rather than a single lucky model.

The honest caveat

Hybrid linear attention is still young. Its behaviour at the extreme end of long context, under heavy quantisation, and on retrieval tasks specifically adversarial to a fixed-size recurrent state is less thoroughly characterised than plain attention after eight years of scrutiny. If your workload is exact recall from a very long context, benchmark it yourself rather than trusting the aggregate scores.
