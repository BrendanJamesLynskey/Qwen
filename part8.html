The Specialist Lines

Qwen is not one model, it is a fleet. Vision-language from 2B to 235B-A22B, an omni-modal model that speaks, embedding and reranking models that topped MTEB, a streaming safety classifier, an image generator with unusually good text rendering, and a speech stack. This deck covers what each is for, how it works, and how it is served.

The unifying design decision

Every model here shares the Qwen tokenizer, the Qwen chat template and (where applicable) a Qwen3 backbone. That is what makes the fleet composable: a Qwen3-Embedding vector, a Qwen3-VL caption and a Qwen3Guard verdict can be assembled into one pipeline without translation layers between them. It is also why prompt-engineering intuitions transfer across the whole set.

And the 2026 twist

Qwen3.5 made the base model natively multimodal, absorbing the separate VL line into the mainline. The Qwen3-VL models remain the right choice when you want a small, dedicated, cheap vision model — a 2B or 4B for OCR at volume — but the flagship vision capability now lives in the general family. Part 09 covers that shift.

02

Qwen3-VL — the lineup and the three innovations

Qwen3-VL spans 2B to 235B-A22B, dense and MoE, each in Instruct and Thinking editions, with a native 256K context covering text and interleaved multimodal input alike. Three named architectural changes distinguish it from Qwen2.5-VL.

03

What Qwen3-VL is actually used for

Document intelligence

The dominant production use. Dense OCR across dozens of languages, table and chart extraction with structure preserved, multi-page reasoning within the 256K window. For a great many teams a Qwen3-VL-8B replaced an entire pipeline of layout detection, OCR engine and post-processing rules.

GUI and computer-use agents

Trained to localise UI elements and emit actions. Combined with a tool layer that clicks and types, this is the perception half of a computer-use agent — and the grounding coordinates are stable enough to act on.

Visual coding

Screenshot or design mock in, working HTML/CSS/JS out. A genuinely useful party trick that turns out to matter: it is the fastest way to convert a legacy interface or a whiteboard sketch into a working prototype.

Video understanding

Long-form video with timestamp-grounded event localisation. Summarise an hour of footage, find the moment something happened, or caption a stream — with the caveat that video is by far the most token-expensive input you can send.

Spatial and 3D grounding

Bounding boxes, points and relative spatial relationships, extended toward 3D reasoning about scenes. Useful for robotics perception and for any task where "which one" matters as much as "what".

Edge OCR at volume

The 2B is the sleeper. It has passed 18 million downloads because it is small enough to run at scale on cheap hardware while still reading documents competently — the highest-volume vision workload there is.

Instruct or Thinking?

Use Instruct for extraction, OCR, captioning and any high-volume perception task — you want a fast, literal reading of the image. Use Thinking when the question requires inference over what is seen: a physics diagram, a chart that needs arithmetic, a multi-step deduction from a schematic. Sending an OCR job to a Thinking model wastes a great many tokens for no accuracy gain.

04

Serving a VLM without running out of memory

Vision-language models are the most common cause of surprise OOM in vLLM, for one structural reason: you no longer control the token count from the prompt. A user uploading a 4000×3000 photograph decides your memory budget for you.

A defensively-configured Qwen3-VL endpoint
# vLLM ≥ 0.11.0 is required for Qwen3-VL
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct \
  --max-model-len 65536 \
  --limit-mm-per-prompt '{"image": 4, "video": 1}' \
  --max-num-seqs 16 \
  --gpu-memory-utilization 0.90

# The MoE flagship — expert parallelism across four GPUs
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct \
  --tensor-parallel-size 4 --enable-expert-parallel \
  --limit-mm-per-prompt '{"image": 8, "video": 2}'

# Text-only workload on a VL checkpoint? Switch the vision path off entirely.
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct --limit-mm-per-prompt '{"image": 0, "video": 0}'

The architecture names to look for in vLLM's supported-model list are Qwen3VLForConditionalGeneration (dense) and Qwen3VLMoeForConditionalGeneration (MoE). If you hit OOM, the ordered remedy is: lower --limit-mm-per-prompt, then --max-model-len, then --max-num-seqs, then --max-num-batched-tokens, and only then reach for a quantised checkpoint.

05

Qwen3-Omni — one model, every modality

September 2025. Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B takes text, images, audio and video, and produces text and streaming speech. It carries forward the Thinker–Talker split from Qwen2.5-Omni onto a Qwen3 MoE backbone.

What it enables

  • Live voice and video conversation, with interruption — the Talker begins speaking from the Thinker's hidden states before the sentence is finished
  • Broad multilingual coverage: text across the full Qwen3 language set, with speech input and output supported over a smaller subset
  • A dedicated captioner variant for detailed audio description
  • Because it is 30B-A3B, real-time interaction is achievable on a single node

What it costs you

  • The hardest thing in the fleet to serve. Audio encoding, video decoding, LLM inference and a streaming speech decoder in one request path
  • Streaming speech output means the response is not a single generation to be batched — it is a real-time pipeline with its own latency budget
  • Serving-stack support is thinner than for text or VL models; check your runtime's coverage before designing around it
  • Text-only quality is competitive but not the family's best — multimodality costs something
When to use Omni, and when not to

Use it when the interaction is inherently multimodal and low-latency: a voice assistant, a live translator, an accessibility tool. Do not use it as a general text model that happens to accept audio — a pipeline of Qwen3-ASR → Qwen3.6 → a TTS model is usually cheaper, easier to serve, easier to debug and better at each individual step. Omni's advantage is end-to-end latency and cross-modal grounding, and if you are not buying those, you are paying for them anyway.

06

Qwen3-Embedding and Qwen3-Reranker

Released June 2025 in 0.6B, 4B and 8B, built on Qwen3 bases. The 8B embedding model took the top position on the MTEB multilingual leaderboard on release with a score of 70.58 — and the pair are designed to be used together, as the two stages of a retrieval cascade.

Both models in vLLM — different runners, not just different weights
# Embedding: a pooling model, exposed on /v1/embeddings
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B --task embed

# Reranker: a scoring model, exposed on /v1/score (or /rerank)
vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-4B --task score

In January 2026 the pair gained multimodal siblings — Qwen3-VL-Embedding and Qwen3-VL-Reranker — which embed and rank images and document pages alongside text, so a scanned page can be retrieved without an OCR step in the ingestion pipeline.

07

Qwen3Guard — safety on the token stream

Released September 2025 in 0.6B, 4B and 8B. Its distinguishing feature is in the subtitle of the announcement: real-time safety for your token stream. Two variants exist, and the streaming one is the interesting engineering.

Qwen3Guard-Gen

The classical shape. A complete prompt or a complete response goes in; a classification comes out. Simple to reason about, easy to log, and adds one full model call of latency at each end of the conversation.

Qwen3Guard-Stream

Classifies as the response is generated, token by token. An unsafe completion can be cut off part-way rather than being generated in full and then discarded. That matters enormously in a streaming UI, where the user has already read what you were about to redact.

Deployment note

The 0.6B is the one to reach for. A guard model runs on every request in both directions, so its cost is pure overhead on your entire traffic. Running an 8B guard in front of a 4B generator is a common and expensive mistake.

08

Qwen-Image and the speech stack

Qwen-Image (Aug 2025)

A 20B MMDiT — multimodal diffusion transformer — for image generation. Its distinguishing capability is text rendering: it draws legible, correctly-spelled text in complex layouts, in Chinese as well as English, which most diffusion models still do badly. Qwen-Image-Edit extended that to editing, and Qwen-Image-3.0 (July 2026) broadened text rendering to 12 languages.

Why text rendering is hard

Diffusion models operate on continuous latents where a letterform is a texture, not a symbol. Getting "SALE 20% OFF" to render with the right glyphs, spacing and kerning requires the model to hold discrete symbolic structure through a continuous denoising process. It is a genuinely different capability from image quality, and it is what makes a generator usable for posters, slides and UI mockups.

Qwen3-ASR

Open speech recognition with multilingual coverage, plus Qwen-Audio-3.0-ASR-Flash (July 2026) tuned for low-latency transcription. The natural front end for a voice pipeline that does not need Omni's end-to-end integration.

Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS and Qwen-MT

TTS (July 2026) covers expressive speech across 16 languages. Qwen-MT (July 2025) is a dedicated translation model over 92 languages and major dialects, reinforcement-tuned for translation quality specifically — considerably cheaper and better at the job than prompting a general model.

The generalist trap

It is tempting to do everything with one large general model. For translation, transcription, embedding, reranking and moderation, a dedicated small Qwen specialist is almost always cheaper, faster and better than a prompt to a big generalist. The general model's job is the part that genuinely needs general intelligence; everything else should be a specialist call.
