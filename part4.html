01

The o1 moment and what it actually changed

In September 2024 OpenAI shipped o1, and the interesting claim was not that it was smarter. It was where the intelligence came from. o1 spent a variable, sometimes very large, number of tokens reasoning privately before answering — and its accuracy rose with that spend.

That reframed the economics of the entire field. Until then, capability was bought at training time: more parameters, more tokens, one fixed cost paid once, and every inference cost the same. o1 showed capability could also be bought at inference time, per request, in proportion to how hard the request was.

Train-time scaling

Cost paid once, by the lab. Fixed capability, uniform latency, predictable serving. The whole 2020–2024 playbook — and the thing scaling laws described.

Test-time scaling

Cost paid per request, by whoever serves it. Capability becomes a dial. Latency becomes unpredictable and varies by an order of magnitude between easy and hard prompts.

o1's reasoning traces were hidden. The open question of late 2024 was whether an open lab could reproduce the capability at all — and whether it needed a frontier-scale model to do it. Two months later, Qwen answered both questions with a 32B.

Why the name

QwQ is "Qwen with Questions". The team framed it explicitly as a model that doubts itself — the release note opened on the idea that genuine reasoning requires holding uncertainty rather than resolving it immediately. That is a fair description of the behaviour, for better and for worse.

02

QwQ-32B-Preview — a preview that behaved like one

Published 28 November 2024: 32.5B parameters on the Qwen2.5-32B architecture, 64 layers, GQA with 40 query heads over 8 KV heads, 32K context, Apache 2.0. The benchmark numbers were startling for the size.

BenchmarkWhat it measuresQwQ-32B-Preview
GPQAGraduate-level science, Google-proof65.2
AIMECompetition mathematics50.0
MATH-500Broad mathematical problem solving90.6
LiveCodeBenchContamination-resistant competitive programming50.0

Those figures put an Apache-2.0 32B ahead of o1-mini and competitive with o1-preview on scientific and mathematical reasoning — from a model you could run on a single 48GB card, four months after the paradigm was announced.

And the failure modes were spectacular

The release note listed them candidly, which is rare and worth respecting:

  • Language mixing. Reasoning traces would drift between English and Chinese mid-derivation, sometimes mid-sentence.
  • Recursive loops. The model could enter circular reasoning — re-deriving the same step, second-guessing, re-deriving — and burn its entire token budget without converging.
  • Uneven common sense. Superb at olympiad geometry, unreliable at questions a 7B instruct model handles without effort. Reasoning capability and general capability had visibly come apart.
  • Safety regressions. The reasoning trace was a channel the safety post-training had not covered.
The structural lesson

Every one of those failures is a symptom of the same thing: reasoning had been trained as a separate skill, on top of a general model, without the two being reconciled. That observation is precisely what motivated the merge into Qwen3 — and, later, the discovery that merging them naively has costs of its own.

03

What “thinking” is, mechanically

There is no separate reasoning module. A thinking model is an ordinary autoregressive decoder that has been trained to emit a long private monologue inside delimiter tokens before it commits to an answer. Everything operational about serving reasoning models follows from that one fact.

vLLM — splitting the trace out of the response
# Serve with a reasoning parser so the trace lands in its own field
vllm serve Qwen/QwQ-32B --reasoning-parser deepseek_r1

# The response then carries both, separately:
{
  "reasoning_content": "Let me check the constraint again...",   # the <think> region
  "content":           "The answer is 42."                     # what you show the user
}

Without a reasoning parser the trace arrives inline in content, delimiters and all, and every downstream consumer — JSON parsers, tool-call extractors, evaluation harnesses — sees it. Setting the parser is not optional in production; Part 11 covers which parser goes with which Qwen generation.

04

QwQ-32B — reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards

The full release on 6 March 2025 kept the 32B size but rebuilt the post-training, and the result matched DeepSeek-R1 — a 671B model with 37B active — on key reasoning benchmarks. That is roughly a twenty-fold reduction in total parameters for comparable reasoning quality, and it is the strongest single argument in the family's history that reasoning is a post-training property, not a scale property.

Context also grew from 32K to 131K, which for a thinking model is not a luxury. A trace of 20,000 tokens against a 32K window leaves very little room for the actual problem, let alone retrieved documents or a conversation history.

05

Test-time compute: the curve and its knee

The practical question a reasoning model forces on you is: how many thinking tokens should I pay for? The published scaling results across the field share a consistent shape — accuracy rises roughly logarithmically with the thinking budget, and then flattens.

What to do with that

  • Set a thinking budget per route, not per application. A classification endpoint and a debugging endpoint should not share one budget.
  • Cap it and mean it. Reasoning models loop. An unbounded budget on a pathological prompt will consume tens of thousands of tokens and return nothing useful.
  • Measure accuracy-per-token, not accuracy. Past the knee you are buying almost nothing at full price.
  • Route, don't toggle globally. The right architecture is a cheap classifier in front of two modes — which is exactly what Qwen3 tried to internalise, with mixed results.
06

QVQ — reasoning over pixels

QVQ-72B-Preview arrived on Christmas Eve 2024, built on Qwen2-VL-72B: the same long-form deliberate reasoning, applied to problems where the premises are in an image. A hosted QVQ-Max followed in March 2025.

Why visual reasoning is a different problem

A text reasoning model re-reads its premises for free — they are tokens in context. A visual reasoner must first extract premises from pixels, and any perception error is silently propagated through every subsequent step. Misread one digit off a diagram and forty steps of flawless algebra produce a confidently wrong answer.

What it was good at

Physics problems posed as diagrams, geometry from figures, chart interpretation with arithmetic, multi-step reasoning over scientific plots. On MMMU it scored around 70 — state of the art for an open model at the time, and a large jump over its own Qwen2-VL-72B base.

image + question ↓ perceive: what is in the figure, literally ↓ transcribe: state the premises in text ↓ reason over the transcribed premises ↓ check the conclusion back against the image

That transcription step is the interesting part. QVQ effectively learned to convert an image into a textual premise set and then reason in the medium it reasons well in. The pattern persists: today's Qwen3-VL-Thinking and Qwen3.5 do the same thing more fluently, and the same failure mode — confident reasoning from a misperception — is still the dominant error class in visual reasoning evaluation.

Practical note

If you deploy a visual reasoner, make it state its perceived premises explicitly before it reasons, and validate those. It is far cheaper to catch "the model read the axis as 10× too large" at the premise stage than to debug a wrong final answer.

07

Serving a reasoning model is a different job

QwQ was where a lot of teams learned that a reasoning model breaks assumptions baked into their serving stack. All of these are still true of Qwen3-Thinking, Qwen3.5 and Qwen3.6 today.

AssumptionWhy it breaksWhat to do instead
Output length is roughly predictableTraces vary from 200 to 30,000+ tokens on the same endpointProvision KV cache for the p95 trace, not the mean; cap max_tokens generously but finitely
Streaming hides latencyNothing user-visible is emitted until </think>Stream the reasoning to a “thinking…” affordance, or accept the wait and say so in the UI
Time-outs can be tightA hard problem legitimately takes minutesPer-route time-outs; a token budget is a better control than a wall-clock limit
Cost tracks visible outputMost tokens billed are never shownMeter reasoning_content separately — it is usually the majority of the bill
Temperature 0 is the safe defaultGreedy decoding makes reasoning models loop badlyUse the model card's sampling — typically temperature 0.6–1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 20
The context window is the context budgetThe trace occupies the same KV cache as your documentsBudget explicitly: prompt + retrieved context + trace + answer ≤ window
The greedy-decoding trap

It is counter-intuitive, but reasoning models must not be run at temperature 0. Greedy decoding maximises the chance of entering a repetition attractor — the model re-derives the same step forever because that continuation is always locally most likely. Every Qwen reasoning model card since QwQ has specified a non-zero temperature for exactly this reason, and it is one of the most common self-inflicted production problems with this family.

08

Why the line was absorbed

QwQ-32B shipped in March 2025. Qwen3 shipped in April 2025 with thinking mode built in, and no QwQ successor was ever released. The decision looks obvious in hindsight; the reasoning behind it is worth being explicit about.

Costs of a separate reasoning model

  • Two models to train, evaluate, align, document and serve
  • Capability divergence — QwQ was worse than Qwen2.5-32B at ordinary tasks despite being better at hard ones
  • The routing decision is pushed onto the user, who usually does not know which mode a prompt needs
  • Two sets of weights resident, or a cold model swap on every route change

The promise of merging

  • One checkpoint, one serving deployment, one safety story
  • Reasoning and general capability trained jointly, so they reinforce rather than compete
  • The mode becomes a request-level parameter — enable_thinking — not an infrastructure decision
  • A single evaluation surface for the whole capability envelope
And it did not entirely work

Qwen3's hybrid thinking mode was the right idea imperfectly executed. Within three months the team had split the flagship back into separate Instruct-2507 and Thinking-2507 checkpoints, because a single model trying to be excellent in both modes was measurably worse in each than a model dedicated to one. Part 05 tells that story in full — it is the most instructive failure in the family's history, and Qwen3.5 eventually solved it properly.

09

What survived into everything since

The QwQ line lasted sixteen weeks and produced two models, neither of which anyone should deploy today. Its legacy is nonetheless present in every Qwen released since.

Techniques that became standard

  • RL with verifiable rewards as the primary reasoning driver — now the backbone of Qwen3-Coder's agentic training and every subsequent reasoning release
  • The <think> delimiter convention, and with it the reasoning-parser layer in vLLM and SGLang
  • Explicit thinking budgets as a first-class request parameter
  • Non-zero sampling temperature as a documented requirement rather than a suggestion

Ideas that were proved

  • Reasoning is post-training, not scale. 32B matched 671B. This single result redirected the open-model field's effort from parameter count to post-training method.
  • Verifiable domains generalise partially. RL on maths and code improves reasoning elsewhere — but not uniformly, which is why stage 2 exists.
  • Thinking is not free and not always worth it. The direct motivation for every subsequent attempt at adaptive or controllable reasoning depth.
