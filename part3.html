Qwen2 — the architecture consolidates

Qwen2 landed on 7 June 2024 with five sizes and a clear brief: stop experimenting with the backbone, standardise it, and put the effort into data and context instead.

Three decisions worth naming

  • GQA on every size, including the 0.5B. Qwen1.5 used multi-head attention on the smaller models; Qwen2 standardised grouped-query attention across the board, cutting KV-cache memory by roughly the head-group ratio. Next slide unpacks the arithmetic.
  • Tied embeddings on the small models. At 0.5B, a 151K-entry vocabulary with untied input and output embeddings is a huge fraction of the parameter budget. Tying them recovers capacity for the actual transformer — which is why Qwen2-0.5B punches so far above its size.
  • 27 additional languages deliberately targeted beyond Chinese and English, with cross-lingual code-switching evaluated explicitly rather than left to chance.
A negative result worth knowing

The Qwen2 technical report notes that scaling pre-training beyond roughly 7 trillion tokens — they tried 12T — produced no significant improvement at these model sizes with that data mixture. The lesson the team drew was that data quality and mixture had become the binding constraint, not volume. Qwen2.5's 18T run was only worthwhile because the filtering pipeline was rebuilt first.

02

Grouped-query attention, and what it buys you

GQA is the single highest-leverage inference optimisation in the entire family, and Qwen2 is where it became universal. The idea is simple: keep all the query heads, but let several of them share one key/value head. Quality barely moves; the KV cache shrinks by the sharing ratio.

That 7× reduction is what makes 128K context practical on a single GPU, and it compounds with batching: KV cache is per-sequence, so a 7× smaller cache means roughly 7× more concurrent requests at the same memory budget. In vLLM terms, it is 7× the number of KV blocks, and therefore 7× the throughput ceiling before preemption starts.

Where this goes next

GQA attacks the per-layer cost of the cache but leaves it growing linearly with sequence length. The next move — taken by Qwen3-Next and made mainline in Qwen3.5 — is to remove the cache from three quarters of the layers entirely by replacing them with linear attention. Part 07 covers that.

03

Qwen2.5 — 18 trillion tokens and a full ladder

Qwen2.5 shipped on 19 September 2024 and is, by deployment count, probably the most successful open model release ever made. Seven dense sizes, one pre-training corpus of roughly 18 trillion tokens, 128K context and 8K generation across the range.

0.5B 1.5B 3B 7B 14B 32B 72B

What actually improved

  • Knowledge. MMLU crossed 85 at the top of the range, HumanEval 85+, MATH 80+ — numbers that had been closed-model territory a year earlier.
  • Structured output. Qwen2.5 was explicitly trained to emit JSON and to follow schema instructions, and to tolerate diverse system prompts without losing role. This is the release where Qwen became genuinely usable as a programmable component rather than a chatbot.
  • Instruction robustness. Long-form generation up to 8K tokens, and much better adherence to condition-setting and role-play system prompts — important for agent scaffolds that lean on the system message.
  • 29 languages supported, with the tokenizer already well suited to most of them.

The two sizes that mattered most

Qwen2.5-7B

The default fine-tuning base for two years of open research. Fits a 16GB card at 8-bit or a 8GB card at 4-bit, trains with LoRA on a single 24GB GPU, and is strong enough that a domain fine-tune is often competitive with a much larger general model.

Qwen2.5-32B

The "one good GPU" sweet spot — roughly 20GB at 4-bit, so it runs on a single 3090/4090 with usable context. For a long stretch this was the strongest model most people could actually self-host, and it anchored the local-LLM community.

Licensing followed the Qwen2 split: most sizes Apache 2.0, the 3B under a research licence, and the 72B flagship under the Tongyi Qianwen licence — the last generation where that mattered.

04

Long context, done properly: YaRN and dual chunk attention

Qwen 1.0 extended context with inference-time tricks over a 2K-trained model, and it showed. Qwen2.5 trained at long context and then extended further with two named, well-understood techniques — both of which you will still find as flags in vLLM today.

YaRN — Yet another RoPE extensioN

A refinement of NTK-aware interpolation. RoPE dimensions are split by wavelength: high-frequency dimensions (which encode local order) are left untouched, low-frequency dimensions (which encode global position) are interpolated, and an attention temperature correction compensates for the entropy change. The result extends context by 4–8× with a short fine-tune, or none at all.

In vLLM: --rope-scaling '{"rope_type":"yarn","factor":4.0,"original_max_position_embeddings":32768}'

DCA — Dual Chunk Attention

Used by Qwen2.5-1M to reach a million tokens. The sequence is partitioned into chunks; positions are remapped so that relative distances within a chunk, between adjacent chunks and across distant chunks each stay inside the range the model was trained on. No position the model sees is ever out of distribution — which is precisely the failure mode naive extrapolation hits.

Qwen2.5-1M (January 2025) released 7B and 14B open models at a genuine one-million-token context, alongside a patched vLLM fork carrying the sparse-attention kernels. The hosted Qwen2.5-Turbo offered the same context commercially. It was a research release more than a production one — but it established that Qwen would treat long context as a first-class capability rather than a spec-sheet number.

05

Qwen2.5-Coder — the model that made local coding viable

Released in November 2024 as a full ladder — 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B and 32B — continued-pretrained on roughly 5.5 trillion tokens of code, code-grounded text and synthetic data, covering 92 programming languages at 128K context.

0.5B – 3B

Inline autocomplete. Small enough to run on the CPU of a developer laptop with sub-100ms latency — the tier where fill-in-the-middle quality matters far more than reasoning.

7B – 14B

The everyday assistant tier. Good enough for function-level generation, refactors and test writing; fits a consumer GPU comfortably at 4-bit with room for real context.

32B

The headline. At release it matched GPT-4o on several code benchmarks — the first time a self-hostable open model was credibly at the closed frontier for coding.

What it was, and what it was not

Qwen2.5-Coder was a code-completion and code-generation model. Give it a function signature and a docstring and it writes the function; give it a file with a hole and it fills the hole. What it could not do — and was never trained to do — was drive a loop: read a repository, form a plan, call a shell tool, read the error, and try again.

The gap that defined the next generation

Between Qwen2.5-Coder and Qwen3-Coder sits the single largest capability jump in the family's history, and it is not about code quality at all. It is about multi-turn agency — planning, tool use, environment feedback and recovery. Qwen3-Coder's post-training ran 20,000 environments in parallel specifically to teach that, and Part 06 takes it apart.

The training-time transformation is simple — take a document, cut out a span, and reorder it as prefix / suffix / middle — but it changes what the model is for. Without it, a completion model can only append; with it, it can edit.

06

Qwen2.5-Math and tool-integrated reasoning

Shipped in 1.5B, 7B and 72B alongside Qwen2.5, the Math line is easy to skip — but it contains the idea that became Qwen3's thinking mode, so it is worth two minutes.

Chain of thought (CoT)

The model reasons in natural language and produces the answer itself. Flexible, and it works for word problems and proofs — but the arithmetic is done by a language model, so it is unreliable at exactly the point where precision matters.

Tool-integrated reasoning (TIR)

The model reasons in natural language and emits Python, which is executed; the result is fed back and reasoning continues. Arithmetic, symbolic algebra and numerical work are delegated to an interpreter that is actually correct.

problem statement ↓ reason: set up the equation ↓ emit Python → execute → observe result ↓ reason: interpret, check, continue ↓ final answer

Look at that loop and you are looking at an agent. The observation is the model's own tool output; the policy is the next reasoning step. Qwen2.5-Math trained this pattern in a narrow domain where correctness is cheap to verify automatically — which is exactly what makes reinforcement learning tractable. That combination, verifiable rewards plus an execution loop, is the engine behind QwQ, Qwen3's thinking mode, and Qwen3-Coder's agentic training alike.

Why maths first

Maths and code are the two domains where a machine can grade a machine. A maths answer can be checked against a reference; a program can be run against tests. Every reasoning breakthrough in this family started in one of those two domains and generalised outward — not by coincidence, but because they are the only places you can generate reward signal at scale.

07

Qwen2.5-VL — dynamic resolution and document AI

Released January 2025 in 3B, 7B, 32B and 72B, Qwen2.5-VL was the generation where the vision line stopped being a research curiosity and became the tool people actually use to parse documents.

The three capabilities that mattered

  • Document parsing. A structured HTML-like output format for documents, preserving layout, tables and reading order — not just a flat OCR dump. This is why Qwen2.5-VL displaced dedicated document-AI pipelines for a lot of teams.
  • Grounding. Bounding boxes and points emitted as JSON, with stable coordinate conventions — making it usable as a perception layer for GUI agents rather than only a captioner.
  • Long video. Absolute time encoding aligned to real timestamps, so "what happened at 14 minutes" is answerable rather than approximated from frame indices.
08

Qwen2.5-Omni — Thinker–Talker

March 2025, in 3B and 7B. Omni takes text, images, audio and video in, and emits text and streaming speech out. The architecture that makes real-time speech output possible is worth understanding, because the same split reappears in Qwen3-Omni.

TMRoPE — time-aligned multimodal RoPE — is the other half of the trick. Video frames and audio samples arrive on different clocks; interleaving them by a shared absolute timebase in the position encoding is what lets the model reason about a video and its soundtrack as one event stream rather than two loosely-associated sequences.

09

Why Qwen2.5 is still everywhere in 2026

Four generations have shipped since. Qwen2.5 nevertheless remains one of the most-downloaded model families on Hugging Face, and it is worth being precise about why — because the reasons are operational, not architectural.

The reasons it persists

  • Maximum tooling maturity. Every quantisation format, every fine-tuning framework, every serving stack, every embedded runtime supports it — and has for two years.
  • Dense, so predictable. No expert routing means uniform latency, trivial sharding and no MoE-specific kernel requirements. For edge and embedded deployment that still matters enormously.
  • Enormous fine-tune ecosystem. If you need a domain model, someone has probably already trained one on a Qwen2.5 base.
  • Frozen and boring. A model that will never change again is a feature when you have qualified a pipeline around it.

Where it now falls down

  • No thinking mode. On hard reasoning it is simply outclassed by anything post-QwQ.
  • Weak agentic behaviour. Tool calling works, but multi-turn recovery, planning and long-horizon task completion were never trained for.
  • Context economics. 128K with full quadratic attention costs far more per token than a 262K hybrid-attention model from 2026.
  • Dense means expensive at scale. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B does 3B of work per token; Qwen2.5-32B does 32B. On a throughput-per-dollar basis that is not close.
Practical guidance

In 2026, choose Qwen2.5 deliberately — for a frozen embedded target, for a mature fine-tune you already own, or for a runtime that cannot do MoE. For anything new, start at Qwen3.5 or Qwen3.6: a 9B from 2026 will generally beat a 32B from 2024 on reasoning, tool use and context, at a fraction of the serving cost.
