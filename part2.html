01

August 2023 — the gap Qwen was released into

To understand why Qwen landed the way it did, remember the state of open weights in mid-2023. Llama 2 had shipped in July under a licence with a 700-million-MAU carve-out and an acceptable-use policy. Its tokenizer had a 32,000-entry vocabulary tuned for English. Chinese, Japanese and Korean text tokenized catastrophically — often one token per byte — which meant a nominal 4K context held perhaps 1,300 Chinese characters and inference cost several times more per unit of meaning.

The Chinese open-model scene existed but was fragmented: ChatGLM, Baichuan, InternLM, MOSS, each with its own tokenizer, chat format and licence, and none with a full size ladder or a credible 70B.

The bet

Alibaba's bet was that the winning open family would be the one that was genuinely bilingual at the tokenizer level, shipped a complete size ladder rather than one hero model, and behaved predictably enough that downstream tooling could standardise on it. All three turned out to be correct, and all three were decided in this generation.

Qwen-7B and Qwen-7B-Chat were published on 3 August 2023, followed by Qwen-VL on the 22nd, Qwen-14B and the Qwen-Agent framework in September, and the Qwen-72B flagship with a Qwen-1.8B edge model at the end of November.

02

Qwen-7B: what was actually in the box

Architecturally, Qwen 1.0 was deliberately unadventurous — a Llama-shaped decoder-only transformer with a handful of considered deviations. That conservatism was the point: it meant every existing inference kernel, every LoRA trainer and every quantisation toolchain worked more or less immediately.

Note the last row carefully. Qwen 1.0's long context was an inference-time trick, not a trained property, and it behaved like one — usable for retrieval-style tasks, unreliable for genuine long-form reasoning. Every generation since has moved more of that capability into pre-training.

03

The tokenizer decision that shaped everything

Qwen started from the cl100k byte-level BPE used by GPT-3.5/4 and extended it with a large set of Chinese and multilingual merges, landing at 151,936 entries — nearly five times Llama 2's vocabulary. That one number propagates into cost, latency, context and quality.

The trade is strongly favourable at 7B and above, and the vocabulary was then carried forward almost unchanged for two and a half years: Qwen2 trimmed it slightly to 151,646, and Qwen2.5, Qwen3 and Qwen3-Next all stayed on that lineage. In practical terms that meant KV-cache sizing, prompt-token budgets and fine-tuning data pipelines ported across generations without retokenisation — a bigger operational gift than it sounds.

The lineage finally broke with Qwen3.5 in February 2026, which expanded the vocabulary to roughly 250,000 entries to support 201 languages, reporting 10–60% better encoding efficiency across most of them. Exactly the same trade as 2023, made again for a much wider set of languages — and, as in 2023, paid for in embedding parameters.

Watch out

The one place tokenizer continuity has broken is the Coder line. Qwen3-Coder introduced new special tokens and reassigned token IDs, and the model cards are explicit that you must use the updated tokenizer — stale caches produce silent, hard-to-debug corruption of tool calls.

04

Scaling out — 1.8B, 14B, 72B and the context tricks

By December 2023 the first generation covered four sizes. The shape of that ladder — a tiny edge model, a laptop model, a single-GPU model and a flagship — is the shape every subsequent generation has repeated.

ModelReleasedTraining tokensPosition in the ladder
Qwen-1.8BNov 2023~2.2TEdge and mobile; research licence only
Qwen-7BAug 2023~2.2TThe community default — the fine-tuning base
Qwen-14BSept 2023~3.0TSingle consumer GPU at 4-bit
Qwen-72BNov 2023~3.0TFlagship; the first credible open Chinese 70B-class model

Three stacked context-extension tricks

Qwen 1.0 trained at a short context and then extended at inference using three techniques applied together — a combination worth understanding because variants of all three are still in use today.

  • NTK-aware interpolation. Rather than uniformly compressing RoPE frequencies (which destroys high-frequency positional detail), scale the base frequency non-uniformly so low-frequency dimensions stretch and high-frequency ones stay sharp. Training-free, and the direct ancestor of YaRN.
  • LogN attention scaling. As sequences grow, attention entropy grows with them and the distribution flattens. Scaling logits by a factor logarithmic in sequence length keeps the sharpness of attention roughly constant, which measurably improves extrapolation quality.
  • Window attention on lower layers. Lower layers are more sensitive to context extension than upper ones, so Qwen restricted them to a local window while letting upper layers attend globally — a cheap layer-wise mitigation, and a conceptual precursor to the hybrid-attention stacks of Qwen3-Next and Qwen3.5.
The lesson that stuck

All three tricks are stopgaps for the same underlying problem: attention over long sequences is expensive and badly conditioned. Qwen has attacked that problem in every generation since — with trained long context in Qwen2.5, with dual chunk attention in Qwen2.5-1M, and finally by replacing three quarters of the attention layers outright in Qwen3.5.

05

Qwen-VL and Qwen-Audio — the multimodal seeds

Nineteen days after Qwen-7B, the team shipped Qwen-VL. That speed was a statement of intent: multimodality was never going to be a bolt-on afterthought for this family, and by Qwen3.5 it would be trained in from the first token.

Qwen-VL (August 2023)

  • OpenCLIP ViT-bigG vision encoder frozen at 448×448 — unusually high resolution for the era
  • A position-aware vision-language adapter: a single-layer cross-attention module compressing the patch grid to a fixed 256 visual tokens, with 2D absolute position encodings preserved so spatial layout survives compression
  • Trained for grounding — bounding boxes emitted as text coordinates in the output stream — and for OCR at a time when most VLMs could not read a receipt

Qwen-Audio (November 2023)

  • Whisper-large-v2-derived audio encoder over a Qwen-7B backbone
  • A multi-task training format covering speech recognition, translation, speaker analysis, and non-speech sound and music understanding in one hierarchy of tags
  • Established the pattern completed by Qwen3-Omni: one backbone, many encoders, a shared token stream

The architectural idea in both is the same and it is worth naming: compress a modality into a small, fixed number of tokens and splice them into the text sequence. Everything downstream — the LLM, the KV cache, the sampler, the serving stack — is unchanged. It is why vLLM can serve a VLM through essentially the same code path as a text model, and why the multimodal line could iterate so fast.

Where this leads

The 256-token bottleneck was also this design's ceiling: a fixed budget cannot represent a dense 4K document scan and a simple photo equally well. Qwen2-VL replaced it with naive dynamic resolution — a variable token count proportional to image size — plus M-RoPE for video. Part 08 follows that thread.

06

Qwen1.5 — the compatibility release

Qwen1.5 arrived on 4 February 2024, and its headline feature was not a capability at all. It was that the architecture had been merged into Hugging Face transformers 4.37, so you could finally load a Qwen without trust_remote_code=True.

That sounds like housekeeping. It was not. trust_remote_code meant arbitrary Python executing at model load, which enterprise security review reliably blocks; it meant every inference server carried a bespoke Qwen code path; and it meant every transformers release risked breaking the family. Upstreaming removed all three problems at once, and the Qwen1.5 downstream ecosystem exploded accordingly.

Nine sizes in one release

0.5B 1.8B 4B 7B 14B 32B 72B 110B MoE-A2.7B
  • 32K context across every size, including the 0.5B — the first time the whole ladder shared a context budget.
  • Qwen1.5-110B was the first Qwen past 100B parameters and, at release, among the strongest open base models available anywhere.
  • Consistent chat template and system-prompt handling across all sizes, with multilingual evaluation broadened well past the original Chinese/English pair.
  • Day-one integration with vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp, Ollama, LMStudio, Axolotl and LLaMA-Factory — the release note read like a compatibility matrix, which was the point.
Engineering read

Qwen1.5 is the clearest demonstration in the whole family that distribution is a model capability. It was, on paper, a modest quality increment over Qwen 1.0. It was nonetheless the release that made Qwen the default open base, because it made Qwen the cheapest open base to adopt.

07

Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B — the first sparsity experiment

In March 2024 the team published a model that mattered far more than its benchmark scores: a 14.3B-parameter mixture-of-experts activating just 2.7B per token, matching Qwen1.5-7B's quality at roughly 1.74× the inference speed and about 25% of the training cost.

Two ideas that became permanent

  • Upcycling. The MoE was not trained from scratch — it was initialised by splitting and replicating the FFN of the existing Qwen-1.8B. That is why it cost a quarter of the compute, and it is a standard technique in the family since.
  • Fine-grained experts with always-on shared experts. Many small experts sharpen specialisation; a few always-active shared experts hold the common knowledge that would otherwise have to be duplicated into every expert. Qwen3-Next runs 512 experts on the same principle.
08

CodeQwen1.5 — the coder line begins

In April 2024 the family forked for the first time. CodeQwen1.5-7B took the Qwen1.5-7B base and continued pre-training it on roughly 3 trillion tokens of code across 92 programming languages.

What it introduced

  • 64K context, double the general model — the first acknowledgement that code needs more context than chat
  • Fill-in-the-middle training, so the model could complete inside a file rather than only append to it — the capability every IDE autocomplete actually needs
  • Repository-level pre-training: files concatenated with path markers so cross-file references were learnable

Why the fork was necessary

Code and natural language compete for capacity at 7B. A general model trained on 5–10% code cannot match a specialist trained on 70–90% code without giving up chat quality. Forking lets each optimise its own mixture — and, crucially, lets the coder line pursue longer contexts and different post-training objectives.

09

What this generation settled, and what it got wrong

Settled — still true in 2026

  • A ~152K byte-level BPE vocabulary, carried essentially unchanged for three years
  • The full size ladder as a release unit, not a single hero model
  • Base + Instruct checkpoints published together
  • Upstream transformers support and day-one vLLM/SGLang recipes as release requirements
  • Fine-grained MoE with always-on shared experts
  • Specialist forks for code and vision that rejoin the mainline later

Got wrong — fixed later

  • Bolt-on long context. Inference-time RoPE tricks over a 2K-trained model produced context that technically worked and practically did not. Fixed by training long in Qwen2.5.
  • The bespoke licence. A source-available licence with a MAU threshold suppressed exactly the commercial adoption the release was chasing. Fixed by Apache 2.0 from Qwen2 onward.
  • Chat-shaped post-training. Qwen1.5 was tuned to answer questions, not to drive tools in a loop. Agentic capability had to be retrofitted, and would not be trained for directly until Qwen3.
  • Fixed visual token budgets. Qwen-VL's 256-token bottleneck capped document understanding; replaced by dynamic resolution in Qwen2-VL.
